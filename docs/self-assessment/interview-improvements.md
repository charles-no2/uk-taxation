# Self Assessment interview improvements

## 19 September 2026 — start with behaviour, not tax labels

### Problem

The opening circumstances question asked people to identify themselves using
tax or legal categories such as “sole trader”, “landlord”, “income from abroad”
and “sold, gave away or exchanged assets”. A person may not know whether an
activity meets one of those definitions, so the question risks both confusion
and missed information.

### Decision

Start the interview with plain descriptions of real-world activity. Ask what
the person did, owned, received or sold during the tax year, then use follow-up
questions to establish the relevant tax treatment. Make clear that selecting
an activity does not itself mean tax is due or that a return is required.

### Proposed opening prompt

“Thinking about 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, did any of these happen? Choose
everything that sounds relevant, and include anything else you are unsure
about: worked for an employer; did paid work independently; ran or owned a
business; received money from a company you own; rented out or owned an
interest in property; received a pension; earned bank interest or investment
income; bought, held, sold or swapped shares, funds, cryptocurrency or other
investments; received money or income from outside the UK; received money from
a trust or an estate; or something else.”

### Interview rule

Use the user’s everyday description first. Only introduce a tax category when
explaining its relevance, define it plainly, and leave uncertain classification
open rather than treating it as a negative answer.

## 19 September 2026 — one question, with context

### Problem

Even when written in plain language, a long opening checklist makes the user
scan many unrelated scenarios and decide which details matter. This is an
unnecessary cognitive load at the point where the interview should establish
trust and momentum.

### Decision

Ask exactly one main question per turn. Introduce it with one short sentence
that says what the answer helps determine, and offer “I’m not sure”. Follow
with the next behaviour-based question only after the answer is recorded. Do
not provide an all-at-once list of income sources unless the user asks for it.

### First question after restart

“First I’ll check whether any work for an employer needs to be included. From
6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, did an employer pay you wages, salary, bonuses,
or other work pay? Yes, no, or I’m not sure.”

## 19 September 2026 — defer document figures to the final folder request

### Decision

When a user confirms they have a certificate or statement, such as a P60, do
not ask them to transcribe its figures during the question-and-answer
interview. Record the document as available in the session, then ask at the
end for one folder containing the relevant supporting documents. Include the
P60 and other applicable records in that final folder checklist.

## 19 September 2026 — use local guidance first and request only relevant records

### Decision

Before checking external sources, search the workspace's tax law and guidance
documents for the applicable rule. Use current official external material only
to resolve a gap or verify that a time-sensitive rule has changed.

Ask for a supporting document only when, on the facts already established, it
could change a Self Assessment return entry or a required check. Do not request
documents merely because an account had activity. In particular, ordinary
interest, income, gains and trades within an ISA are tax-free and are not
declared, so ISA statements are not part of the folder checklist unless a
separate, relevant issue has been identified.

## 19 September 2026 — retain every web source that supports an answer

### Decision

If a web search result is used to reach or support a conclusion given to the
user, retain the exact official page under `docs/` and make it discoverable
locally. Do not leave a search result or a chat citation as the only trace of
the source.

For each such source:

1. save the retrieved page or source file under the relevant `docs/` topic;
2. add the direct official URL, local path, retrieval date and purpose to
   `docs/data-sources.md` (the source record);
3. update the relevant local index or summary so a later session can find it
   without another web search; and
4. update `docs/interactive-workflow.md` when the source materially changes a
   question, branch, required record, return entry or specialist-review route.

The 19 September 2026 cryptoasset receipt guidance is the first retained source
under this rule.

## 19 September 2026 — do not leave a question open-ended without examples

### Decision

Every question that asks the user to identify a type of arrangement, payment,
benefit, asset or record must include a few concrete, everyday examples and
the option “I’m not sure”. Do not rely on a broad prompt such as “What type of
insurance?” when the user may not know the category name.

Examples must be illustrative rather than exhaustive and should be selected to
help the user recognise the relevant facts without implying that any example is
automatically taxable or reportable.
