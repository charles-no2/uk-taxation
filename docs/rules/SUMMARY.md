# How the main tax rules work

These are explanations of tax mechanisms, not a catalogue of Acts. The
[guidance summary](../guidance/SUMMARY.md) expands the personal-income categories.
The scope is the **2025–26 individual-return workflow**, with other taxes kept
separate. Do not treat revised legislation as a year-specific rate table:
commencement, jurisdiction and amendments still need checking.

## 1. Income Tax: calculate each source, then combine them

Employment earnings, trading profit and rental profit are computed differently,
but they feed one individual's annual Income Tax calculation. Eligible deductions
and allowances reduce the income exposed to tax. The remaining slices are charged
at the applicable rates; tax reductions and additional charges affect liability.
Tax withheld is credited against the bill, not deducted as a business expense.

This means three income sources do not normally create three Personal Allowances.
It also means a £1,000 expense deduction and a £1,000 tax credit are not equivalent:
the former saves the tax on £1,000, while the latter acts on the tax itself.

Nuances: savings and dividends have special ordering and nil-rate bands; personal
allowances can taper; losses and investment reliefs can require claims with limits.
Core calculation rules: [Income Tax Act 2007, Parts 2–4 and 8](income-tax/income-tax-act-2007.txt).
Employment charges and deductions: [2003 Act, Parts 2–5](income-tax/income-tax-earnings-and-pensions-act-2003.txt).
Trading, property and investment charges: [2005 Act, Parts 2–5](income-tax/income-tax-trading-and-other-income-act-2005.txt).

## 2. Business expenditure: allowable costs reduce profit

Business profit is not simply all bank deposits minus all withdrawals. Receipts
and expenses must belong to the taxable activity and the appropriate period.
Private spending is excluded. Cash-basis and traditional accounting can recognise
income and expenditure at different times.

Capital allowances relieve qualifying asset expenditure against profit, sometimes
immediately and sometimes over time. They are not a reimbursement of the asset's
price. Accounting depreciation is not itself a tax allowance. Private use,
disposals and cessation can restrict or adjust relief.

Nuances: cash-basis equipment expenses, cars, asset pools and buildings differ.
Individual residential-landlord finance costs generally receive restricted tax
reductions rather than ordinary expense deductions; losses are not universally
offsettable against every other income source. Details:
[Capital Allowances Act 2001, Part 2](income-tax/capital-allowances-act-2001.txt),
[trading and property provisions](income-tax/income-tax-trading-and-other-income-act-2005.txt),
[loss relief, Income Tax Act 2007 Part 4](income-tax/income-tax-act-2007.txt).

## 3. Capital Gains Tax: disposal value less allowable cost

Capital Gains Tax generally charges realised gains on disposals of chargeable
assets. A sale, exchange or gift can trigger the calculation. Disposal value less
allowable acquisition costs and qualifying expenditure produces the starting gain;
losses, reliefs and an available annual exemption affect the chargeable total.

An asset bought for £8,000 and sold for £11,000 has a starting gain of £3,000, not
£11,000, before other costs and reliefs. In specified cases a gift uses market
value despite no cash being received. Ordinary unrealised investment growth does
not itself trigger an annual disposal gain.

Nuances: connected persons, spouse transfers, main-home relief, share matching,
non-residents and temporary non-residence change the analysis. Details:
[Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992](capital-gains/taxation-of-chargeable-gains-act-1992.txt),
[precise provision locations](capital-gains/taxation-of-chargeable-gains-act-1992.index.md),
[saved calculation and reporting explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/capital-gains-tax/index.txt).

## 4. Pensions: contribution relief does not eliminate every tax charge

Eligible pension contributions can receive Income Tax relief. That is separate
from the annual-allowance test of pension saving, which can include employer
contributions and defined-benefit growth. Excess saving may create a charge even
when the contribution otherwise qualifies for relief.

Pension income paid out is generally taxable. Qualifying lump sums may be tax-free
within limits; unauthorised payments can trigger special charges. Money going in,
growth for allowance purposes and money coming out are therefore separate checks.

Nuances: earnings limits, carry-forward, tapering, flexible access and overseas
transfers need separate treatment. Details: [Finance Act 2004 Part 4,
especially reliefs and charges in Chapters 4–5](pensions/finance-act-2004.txt),
[pension income in the 2003 Act Part 9](income-tax/income-tax-earnings-and-pensions-act-2003.txt),
[saved contribution explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/tax-on-your-private-pension/index.txt).

## 5. National Insurance: contributions on relevant earnings or profits

National Insurance uses its own contribution base and thresholds rather than
the Income Tax Personal Allowance. Employees generally pay Class 1 through payroll;
employers have a separate liability. Self-employed people generally pay Class 4
on relevant profits. Investment income is not automatically subject to those classes.

For 2025–26, eligible self-employed people with sufficient profits receive
treated-as-paid Class 2 without a cash payment; lower-profit cases may choose
voluntary contributions. Contributions and credits can protect benefit entitlement.

Nuances: age, multiple jobs, overseas work and mixed employment/self-employment
matter. The [Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992 Part I](national-insurance/social-security-contributions-and-benefits-act-1992.txt)
provides the core framework; regulations, annual changes and territorial
counterparts also matter. See [saved contribution guidance](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/self-employed-national-insurance-rates/index.txt).

## 6. Inheritance Tax: chargeable transfers and estate value

Inheritance Tax concerns transfers of wealth rather than annual earnings.
Broadly, relevant assets less allowable debts establish the value considered;
exemptions, reliefs and nil-rate bands determine the taxable remainder.
Lifetime gifts can affect available bands or create charges.

Many outright gifts to individuals become exempt if the donor survives seven
years. That is not a blanket rule for gifts into trusts or property the donor
continues to enjoy. Trusts can also incur periodic or exit charges. Inheriting
capital does not make it ordinary taxable salary of the beneficiary.

Nuances: spouse and charity exemptions, transferable bands, qualifying homes,
business/agricultural assets and overseas connections have conditions.
Details: [Inheritance Tax Act 1984](inheritance-tax/inheritance-tax-act-1984.txt),
[saved inheritance and gift explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/inheritance-tax/index.txt).
Not every relevant anti-avoidance provision is in the downloaded Act.

## 7. Value Added Tax: tax on supplies, not business profit

Value Added Tax (VAT) generally applies to taxable supplies by registered
businesses. Under ordinary accounting, output VAT charged on sales is reduced by
recoverable input VAT on purchases. The difference is paid to HMRC, or an excess
of recoverable input tax may be refunded.

For example, £2,000 output VAT less £600 fully recoverable input VAT leaves £1,400
payable. This is not the Income Tax calculation of business profit.
Registration normally depends on taxable turnover tests, not profit or the
individual's total income. VAT returns are separate from Self Assessment.

Zero-rated supplies are taxable at a zero rate. Exempt supplies are different and
can restrict recovery of input tax. Private use, mixed taxable/exempt activities,
cross-border transactions and special schemes can change the basic calculation.

Nuance references: [Value Added Tax Act 1994](vat/value-added-tax-act-1994.txt),
including supply, input-tax, registration and rate provisions;
[saved VAT explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/how-vat-works/index.txt).
The legislation collection is not a complete operational VAT manual.

## 8. Property-purchase taxes: a charge on acquiring land interests

Property-purchase taxes generally charge the buyer on the consideration for
acquiring a chargeable land interest. They do not tax subsequent rent or the
seller's gain. Consideration can include assumed debt rather than only cash.
Ordinary purchase calculations commonly charge bands on slices of value;
leases and special transactions can have additional calculations.

Three separate systems apply: Stamp Duty Land Tax in England and Northern Ireland,
Land and Buildings Transaction Tax in Scotland, and Land Transaction Tax in Wales.
Their rates, reliefs and administration are not interchangeable. An acquisition
can need its own return and payment independently of an annual personal return.

Nuances: additional dwellings, replacing a main residence, linked transactions,
mixed or non-residential property, leases and buyer status can alter the charge.
Do not assume a relief in one jurisdiction exists in another.

- England and Northern Ireland: [Finance Act 2003 Part 4 and associated schedules](property-transactions/finance-act-2003-sdlt-england-northern-ireland.txt);
  [saved Stamp Duty Land Tax explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/stamp-duty-land-tax/index.txt).
- Scotland: [Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (Scotland) Act 2013](property-transactions/land-and-buildings-transaction-tax-scotland-act-2013.txt),
  including consideration and additional-dwelling provisions.
- Wales: [Land Transaction Tax and Anti-avoidance of Devolved Taxes (Wales) Act 2017](property-transactions/land-transaction-tax-wales-act-2017.txt),
  including linked transactions, cross-border land and relief conditions.

## 9. Administration: liability, filing and payment are distinct

Tax law can require notification, records and a return before the final bill is
known. Self Assessment calculates relevant annual liabilities and reconciles tax
already paid. A balancing payment settles the remaining amount; payments on
account advance money towards a later year, rather than charging the same income twice.

Taxpayer amendments, HMRC corrections and enquiries are different procedures.
Late filing and late payment can have separate consequences. No tax payable does
not automatically cancel an issued requirement to file.

Nuances: claim time limits, penalties, interest, digital reporting and separate
disposal returns can matter. Core return and amendment rules:
[Taxes Management Act 1970, sections 7–9A and related provisions](tax-administration/taxes-management-act-1970.txt).
Payment reconciliation: [saved bill explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/understand-self-assessment-bill/index.txt).
The local collection does not contain every modern reporting or penalty provision.
