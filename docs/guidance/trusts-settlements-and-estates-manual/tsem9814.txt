Property held jointly by married couples or civil partners: The 50/50 rule: 50/50 rule and exclusions

Income from property held jointly by married couples and civil partners is treated as beneficially owned by the individuals in equal shares under ITA/S836. Consequently they are taxable on the income 50/50. This rule applies even if the individuals own the property in unequal shares. It can be disapplied only by a declaration on form 17 under ITA/S837. Full details of how the 50/50 rule operates in practice are given at TSEM9828 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9828)-9840.

There are some specific exclusions from the 50/50 rule about income. The 50/50 rule does not apply to

- income to which neither of the individuals is beneficially entitled (TSEM9816 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9816))

- partnership income (TSEM9818 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9818))

- income arising from the commercial letting of furnished holiday accommodation (TSEM9820 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9820))

- income from jointly held shares in a close company (TSEM9822 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9822))

- TSEM9824 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9824)

- income in relation to which a declaration by the individuals under section 837 has effect (unequal beneficial interests) (TSEM9826 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9826))
