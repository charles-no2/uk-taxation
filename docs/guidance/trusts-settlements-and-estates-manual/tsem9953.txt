Ownership and income tax: Specific types of property: bank and building society accounts: example 12: joint names of civil partners - form 17 rule applies
Identifier: TSEM9953
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9953
Published: 2016-05-02T17:04:30+01:00
Updated: 2018-12-14T01:45:23+00:00

A building society account is held in the name of A and B, who are civil partners and live together.

B is severely disabled. She is the sole provider of the funds in the account. Her partner is named on the account so that she can operate it on B’s behalf. A and B submit a form 17 (TSEM9842 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9842)) claiming to have all the interest taxed on B, instead of 50/50 under S836 (TSEM9814 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9814)). The claim is valid and in date, and A submits satisfactory evidence in support of the claim. B is taxable on all the interest.
