Ownership and income tax: Specific types of property: shares: example 3: sole name - Settlements legislation
Identifier: TSEM9973

A and B are a married couple. A is a higher rate taxpayer, and B has no taxable income.

A purchased 100 shares in company Y plc in her sole name. A does not return the dividends, and says that she has transferred the right to all the income to B.

The 50/50 rule (TSEM9814 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9814)) does not apply, because the shares are not held in joint names.

There is a valid deed of assignment showing that A has transferred the beneficial interest in the income to B, but the Settlements legislation applies to deem the dividends to belong to A. A has ‘retained an interest’ if the property or income may be applied for the benefit of A or A’s spouse (TSEM4200 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem4200)). The dividends are taxable on A.
