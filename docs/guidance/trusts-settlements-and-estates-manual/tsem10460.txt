Non-resident trusts: beneficiary’s chargeability - capital distributions made to beneficiary
Identifier: TSEM10460
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem10460
Published: 2016-05-02T16:27:04+01:00
Updated: 2024-06-20T13:39:13+01:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T16:21:22.468414+00:00

If capital distributions are made to a beneficiary - not being the settlor, or his spouse or civil partner - by the trustees of a non-resident trust, you need to consider:

- the application of the settlements legislation ITTOIA05/S629, where the beneficiary is a minor unmarried child of the settlor (TSEM4300 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem4300));

- the application of the Transfer of Assets Abroad legislation ITA07/S731 (INTM601400 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/international-manual/intm601400)) where income payable to a person abroad (which includes trustees of a non-resident trust and any non-resident company underlying a trust) may be chargeable to tax on the beneficiary;

- the application of the Capital Gains Tax anti-avoidance legislation TCGA92/S87 (CG38570c (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg38570c)) where capital gains of the non-resident trust have not been charged to Capital Gains Tax on the trustees (TSEM10265 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem10265)), and may be attributed to the beneficiary as a result of the capital payment.

A non-resident trust may give details of such capital distributions on, for example, a completed trust Return or form 50(FS) (TSEM10130 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem10130)).
