Sections 338 to 339 ITEPA 2003

Section 338 ITEPA 2003 allows a deduction for the full cost of travel for necessary attendance. Where employees use their own vehicle or bicycle for business travel a deduction is given at statutory mileage allowance relief rates and not for actual costs, see EIM31240 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31240).

Travel for necessary attendance is journeys that employees make to or from a place they have to attend in the performance of their duties, but not journeys that are ordinary commuting or private travel.

A deduction is only due where it is necessary (in a real sense, see EIM31645 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31645)) for the employee to attend the particular place on that occasion to perform the duties of their employment, see EIM32270 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32270).

No deduction is due for ordinary commuting. Ordinary commuting generally means travel between home and a permanent workplace but is defined in more detail at EIM32055 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32055). Permanent workplace is defined at EIM32065 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32065). Ordinary commuting includes travel that is substantially ordinary commuting, see EIM32300 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32300).

No deduction is due for private travel. Private travel generally means travel for private rather than work purposes but is defined in more detail at EIM32180 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32180). Private travel includes travel that is substantially private travel, see EIM32320 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32320).
