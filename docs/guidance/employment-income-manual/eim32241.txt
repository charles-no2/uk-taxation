Travel expenses: travel for necessary attendance:  emergency call-out and stand-by: emergency call-out: example
Identifier: EIM32241
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32241
Published: 2014-05-22T10:00:00+01:00
Updated: 2015-10-02T13:19:14+01:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T14:21:57.103161+00:00

An employee is required to be a keyholder for her permanent workplace. One night she is called out by the police responding to a burglar alarm. No deduction is due for the cost of travel between her home and her permanent workplace.

The travel is ordinary commuting, see EIM32055 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32055). It does not cease to be ordinary commuting because it is not made during her ordinary working hours, see EIM32240 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32240).
