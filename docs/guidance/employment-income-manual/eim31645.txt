The general rule for employees: expenses: necessarily incurred
Identifier: EIM31645

The preceding paragraphs establish that the general rule for employees’ expenses in section 336 ITEPA 2003 imposes an objective test; an expense can only be deductible if it is one that each and every holder of an employment would be obliged to incur. A deductible expense must also be one that is “necessarily” imposed on the holder of the employment by the duties of that employment.

To see whether an expense has been necessarily incurred you must find out what tasks have to be undertaken to carry out the duties of the job, see EIM31635 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31635). An expense is only necessary if each and every holder of that employment would have to incur expenditure of that type.

Once again, just as for EIM31642  (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31642)this test relates to the nature of the expense and not to the amount. For example, a business trip may be made by train, car or bus. As long as the trip is necessary it does not matter that the various modes of transport have different costs. If the journey is necessary you should not attempt to restrict the deduction to the minimum cost that could have been incurred.

The case of White v Higginbottom (57TC283) provides a useful example of the practical operation of the “necessary” test, albeit in relation to the similar rule relating to capital allowances, see EIM36550 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim36550).

The effect of this rule is illustrated by example EIM31648 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31648).
