The general rule for employees’ expenses is very restrictive. All of the tests imposed by section 336 ITEPA 2003 must be passed before a deduction is obtained. The tests are stringent and exacting, see EIM31636 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31636). It is important that you get all of the facts before you make a decision, see EIM31635 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31635).

A deduction should only be given to the extent that an expense has actually been incurred. This is illustrated by examples at EIM31631 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31631), EIM31632  (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31632)and EIM31633 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31633).

The rules for travelling expenses are different to the general rule for employees’ expenses, see EIM31810 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31810).

The tests that apply before a deduction can be permitted under section 336 are

- the expense must be one that each and every holder of that employment would have to incur, see EIM31640 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31640)

- the expense must be necessarily incurred, see EIM31645 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31645)

- the expense must be incurred in the performance of the duties of the employment, see EIM31650 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31650)

- the expense must be incurred and paid , see EIM31655 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31655)

- the expense must be wholly and exclusively so incurred, see EIM31660 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31660)

There is a useful summary of these tests in paragraph 8 of the judgment of Lightman J.in Ansell v Brown (73TC338).
