Non-approved schemes: example: non-cash receipts
Identifier: EIM15432
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim15432
Published: 2014-05-22T10:00:00+01:00
Updated: 2015-10-02T11:31:27+01:00

On 1 July 2005, a company marks a director’s retirement by giving him a car valued at £25,000. The receipt falls before 6 April 2006 so the rules relating to non-approved schemes apply and not the rules relating to employer-financed schemes (see EIM15010 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim15010)).

The receipt is in non-cash form and so is not a ‘relevant benefit’ for that purpose (see EIM15403 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim15403) and EIM15421 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim15421)). It follows that the receipt is not from a non-approved “retirement benefits scheme” as defined in EIM15402 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim15402). So no charge under s394 ITEPA 2003 arises.

If, as well as giving the car, the employer gave cash, then the scheme would include a ‘‘relevant benefit’” - because the cash is a lump sum given on retirement (see the definition in EIM15402 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim15402)). Because the scheme includes a ‘relevant benefit’ (the cash lump sum) it is within the definition of a non-approved retirement benefits scheme. It follows that Section 394 ITEPA 2003 taxes both the cash and the value of the car (see EIM15421 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim15421) for guidance on determining that value).
