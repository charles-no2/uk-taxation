Travel expenses: travel for necessary attendance:  safeguards against abuse: the necessary attendance rule:  example
Identifier: EIM32271
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32271
Published: 2014-05-22T10:00:00+01:00
Updated: 2015-10-02T13:19:14+01:00

A technical writer normally works at his employer’s head office. However, to enable him to look after an aunt who is unwell, his employer tells him to work at his aunt’s home for a few weeks.

The employee’s attendance at his aunt’s home is not an objective requirement of the duties of his employment, see EIM32270 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32270). Even though he works from his aunt’s home it is not a workplace, see EIM32055 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32055). Therefore, no deduction is due for the cost of travel between his home and his aunt’s home.
