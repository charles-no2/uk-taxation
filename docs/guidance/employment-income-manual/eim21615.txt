Sections 316 and 316ZA ITEPA 2003

Telephone lines

Some benefits that are work related supplies and services, provided other than on the employer's premises, are exempt from tax (see EIM21611 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim21611)).

From 6 April 2026, where an employee incurs expenditure on a telephone line on behalf of the employer and the employer pays or reimburses those expenses, the exemption under Section 316ZA may apply if the statutory conditions are met (see EIM (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim21619)21619 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim21619)).

These exemptions may apply to a telephone line in the employee's home if:

- there is a clear business need for the employer to provide the employee with a telephone, for instance if making and receiving telephone calls from the home are vital and central parts of the employee's duties

- the employer has procedures to monitor, control and minimise the cost to him of private use

- the employer has no intention of rewarding the employee

The employer may control and minimise the cost to him of private use by either:

- recovering from the employee the cost of private calls or

- ensuring that the cost of any private calls is kept to a minimum, for instance only allowing the employee to make local or off peak calls in clearly defined circumstances

If the first bullet applies (recovering private costs) only the provision of the telephone line, or the reimbursement of the line rental, will be exempt under section 316 or 316ZA ITEPA 2003. The benefit of the calls, both business and private will not be exempt, although the amount the employee makes good (see EIM21120 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim21120)) in respect of the private calls will reduce the cash equivalent of the benefit (see EIM21101 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim21101)) and the employee may claim a deduction for calls made in the performance of his duties.

Alternatively, if the business calls are covered by a dispensation (see EIM30051 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim30051) or EIM30200  (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim30200)for 2016 to 2017 onwards) and the full cost of private calls is made good by the employee, there is no need to report a benefit on form P11D or to claim a deduction.

If the second bullet applies (minimal private use) both the line and the calls may be exempt if the cost of private calls is not significant compared to the total costs of the line and calls.

See EIM21616 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim21616) for examples of employments where the benefit of a telephone line is likely to be exempt.
