Sections 361 to 365 ITEPA 2003

EIM31611 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31611) lists the circumstances in which a deduction may be available for certain expenses that an employee incurs and pays out of general earnings. The employee may also be treated as having received earnings from the employment where amounts are treated as earnings under the benefits code, including:

- sums in respect of expenses, sections 70 to 72 ITEPA 2003, see EIM20601 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim20601), or

- vouchers or credit tokens, sections 73 to 96 ITEPA 2003, see EIM16000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim16000), or

- living accommodation, sections 97 to 113 ITEPA 2003, see EIM11300 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim11300), or

- employment-related benefits, sections 201 to 210 ITEPA 2003, see EIM20001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim20001).

A sum received by an employee as a reimbursement of expenses, or an expense paid on the employee’s behalf, may be treated as general earnings. A deduction is permitted from that charge to the same extent as it would have been permitted if the expense had been paid by the employee or the reimbursement had not been made. For an example of a deduction from an expense, see example EIM31616 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31616).

Similarly, where an amount is charged as general earnings by the benefits code, a deduction is permitted to the extent that a deduction would have been permitted if the employee had incurred and paid an equivalent expense. For an example of a deduction from a benefit, see example EIM31617 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31617). For an example of a deduction from a living accommodation charge, see example EIM31618 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim31618).
