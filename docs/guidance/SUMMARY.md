# How UK personal taxation works

This explains the taxes themselves: what is charged, how the amount is worked
out, and which common exceptions can change the answer. References beside the
nuances lead to the detailed rules.

Scope: the **2025–26 individual-return workflow**, at a conceptual level.
Manuals were downloaded on 18 September 2026; supplementary public explanations
are retained in the [19 September snapshot](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/INDEX.md).
Those rolling pages can show later-year rates. Use year-specific rates and
thresholds before calculating a bill. Examples below are fictional arithmetic,
not eligibility decisions.

## 1. Income Tax: combine taxable income, then apply allowances and bands

Employment pay, trading profit, rental profit, pensions, interest and dividends
are income categories, not independent taxes with separate Personal Allowances.
Calculate the taxable amount from each source, exclude exempt income, apply
eligible deductions and allowances, and charge the remaining slices at the
appropriate rates. Crossing a tax band does not put all income at the higher rate.

In the ordinary 2025–26 calculation, non-savings income occupies bands before
savings, with dividends above those. Savings and dividends have special nil-rate
provisions, and dividends have separate rates. Tax already collected is credited
against liability, not subtracted from gross income.

For example, £28,000 taxable employment earnings plus £7,000 rental profit is
£35,000 income before personal reliefs, not two separately sheltered amounts.
An expense deduction reduces taxable income; a tax reduction acts on the bill.

Nuances: the Personal Allowance can taper with adjusted net income (income after
specified adjustments); Scottish non-savings bands differ; Welsh rates must also
be checked. Pension contributions and Gift Aid can affect allowance and charge
calculations. Details: [saved Income Tax explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/income-tax/index.txt),
[saved allowances explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/income-tax-rates/index.txt),
[Income Tax Act 2007, Part 2](../rules/income-tax/income-tax-act-2007.txt).

## 2. Employment: taxable pay and benefits, less permitted deductions

Salary, wages and bonuses generally count as employment income. Some non-cash
benefits are taxable too, even though no extra money reaches the employee's bank.
Employers usually withhold Income Tax through Pay As You Earn (PAYE). The annual
calculation reconciles that withholding with total income and reliefs.

Report taxable pay and tax deducted separately: take-home pay is not the taxable
amount. Benefits may have statutory values different from their actual cost.
Employee expense relief is restricted; ordinary commuting is generally excluded.

Nuances: cars, accommodation and loans have special valuation rules; termination
packages can contain differently taxed components. Overseas duties, share awards
and salary sacrifice need separate checks. Details:
[benefits](employment-income-manual/eim20000.txt),
[travel](employment-income-manual/eim31800.txt),
[termination payments](employment-income-manual/eim12800.txt),
[overseas employment](employment-income-manual/eim43500.txt).

## 3. Self-employment: tax on profit, not turnover or drawings

A sole trader generally pays Income Tax on business receipts less allowable
business costs, with accounting and tax adjustments. Drawing money for personal
use is not an expense; leaving profit in the business does not make it untaxable.

Cash-basis accounting starts from money received and paid. Traditional accounting
recognises income and expenses under accounting principles, so unpaid invoices
and stock can matter. Private expenditure is excluded; a separable business share
of a mixed expense may be deductible. Asset purchases require their own treatment.

For example, £30,000 receipts less £8,000 wholly allowable running costs gives
£22,000 profit before other adjustments. Withdrawing £15,000 does not change that
profit. It joins other taxable income, and National Insurance may also apply.

Nuances: whether an activity is a trade depends on its facts. The trading allowance
can replace expense deductions, not simply supplement them. Loss claims and
non-tax-year accounts can change the reported amount. Details:
[trade classification](business-income-manual/bim20000.txt),
[cash basis](business-income-manual/bim70000.txt),
[assessment periods](business-income-manual/bim80000.txt),
[saved expense rules](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/index.txt).

## 4. Capital allowances: relief for qualifying business assets

Capital allowances reduce taxable business profits for qualifying expenditure;
they are not a reimbursement of the purchase price. Immediate allowances can
give relief upfront; writing-down allowances spread relief over time.
Accounting depreciation is not itself the tax deduction in a traditional
profit calculation.

The asset, accounting method and business use determine the route. Cash-basis
rules instead treat many equipment purchases as expenses; cars have special
rules. Private use can restrict relief and disposal can adjust the calculation.
A £1,000 deduction saves tax on £1,000 of profit, not automatically £1,000 tax.

Nuances: asset pools, cars, buildings and company-only reliefs differ. Details:
[plant and machinery](capital-allowances-manual/ca20000.txt),
[buildings](capital-allowances-manual/ca90000.txt),
[saved allowance explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/capital-allowances/index.txt).

## 5. Property income: rental profit, with separate finance-cost relief

An individual landlord generally pays Income Tax on rental profit, not gross
rent or the property's increase in value. Allowable running costs reduce rents.
UK lettings are generally combined into a UK property business; overseas lettings
form a separate overseas property business. Ownership determines whose share is taxed.

Repairs and capital improvements are not necessarily treated alike. Mortgage
principal repayments are not deductible rental expenses. Restricted residential
finance costs generally produce a basic-rate tax reduction, subject to limits,
rather than reducing the individual's rental profit. This can also affect
income-based allowance and charge calculations.

For example, £15,000 rent less £3,000 allowable non-finance costs gives £12,000
profit before other adjustments. Residential mortgage interest is then considered
under the finance-cost rules, not automatically subtracted from £12,000.

Nuances: joint ownership, cash-basis eligibility, losses and unused finance costs
need checks. Rent a Room and the property allowance have conditions and are not
extra deductions available alongside every expense claim. The special furnished
holiday lettings regime ended for Income Tax from 6 April 2025.
Details: [finance costs](property-income-manual/pim2050.txt),
[joint ownership](property-income-manual/pim1035.txt),
[losses](property-income-manual/pim4200.txt),
[Rent a Room](property-income-manual/pim4000.txt),
[holiday lettings](property-income-manual/pim4100.txt),
[saved rental calculation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/guidance__income-tax-when-you-rent-out-a-property-working-out-your-rental-income/index.txt).

## 6. Interest and dividends: income with special allowances and rates

Interest outside exempt accounts generally enters the savings-income calculation.
An unused Personal Allowance, the starting rate for savings and the Personal
Savings Allowance can reduce or eliminate tax, depending on other income.
There is not a new general Personal Allowance for each account.

Dividends are company distributions, not employment pay. Amounts above available
allowances are taxed at dividend rates according to the person's bands.
The dividend allowance is a nil-rate band: covered dividends can still occupy
tax bands. Interest and dividends within an Individual Savings Account (ISA)
are normally exempt.

Selling shares is a separate capital-gains event, so the same portfolio can
produce both income and disposal gains in one year.

Nuances: foreign withholding may not settle the UK liability; discounted
securities, accrued income and some investment products have special rules.
Details: [interest](savings-and-investment-manual/saim2000.txt),
[distributions](savings-and-investment-manual/saim5000.txt),
[accrued income](savings-and-investment-manual/saim4000.txt),
[saved dividend explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/tax-on-dividends/index.txt),
[saved interest explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/apply-tax-free-interest-on-savings/index.txt).

## 7. Capital Gains Tax: tax on a disposal gain, not sale proceeds

Capital Gains Tax generally arises on disposal, not just because an investment's
price rises. Sales, exchanges and gifts can count as disposals. Start with
disposal value less allowable acquisition cost, eligible improvements and
incidental transaction costs. Market value replaces actual price in specified cases.

Allowable losses, reliefs and an available annual exempt amount affect the
chargeable total. Rates can depend on income, asset type and disposal date.
For example, £14,000 proceeds less £10,000 allowable cost and £500 eligible fees
gives a £3,500 gain before losses, reliefs and the exemption.

Nuances: main-home relief, spouse transfers, connected-person transactions and
share pooling/matching can change the result. Certain UK property disposals need
reporting/payment before the annual return. Details:
[computation](capital-gains-manual/cg10200c.txt),
[shares](capital-gains-manual/cg50200c.txt),
[land](capital-gains-manual/cg70200c.txt),
[reliefs](capital-gains-manual/cg60201c.txt),
[saved gains explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/capital-gains-tax/index.txt).

## 8. Cryptoassets: earning tokens and disposing of them are different events

There is no single separate “crypto tax”. Investment disposals usually enter
Capital Gains Tax; tokens received as pay or rewards can create income on receipt.
A later disposal can create a further gain or loss. A value already recognised
as income generally contributes to the acquisition cost for the later calculation.

Exchanging one token for another can be a disposal without withdrawing pounds.
Calculations need sterling transaction values, not merely net exchange deposits
and withdrawals. A genuine financial trade can instead bring trading-income rules
into play.

Nuances: mining, airdrops, staking, beneficial ownership in lending arrangements
and token matching require separate checks. Details:
[classification](cryptoassets-manual/crypto20050.txt),
[income](cryptoassets-manual/crypto21000.txt),
[gains](cryptoassets-manual/crypto22000.txt),
[lending and staking](cryptoassets-manual/crypto61000.txt).

## 9. Pensions: contribution relief, allowance charges and withdrawals

Eligible contributions can receive Income Tax relief, but its delivery matters.
Net-pay arrangements deduct employee contributions before calculating Income Tax.
With relief at source, the provider adds basic-rate relief and further relief may
need claiming. Do not claim again for relief already delivered through payroll.

The annual allowance separately tests pension saving: employer contributions and
defined-benefit growth can count. An excess can create a tax charge even when
contributions otherwise qualify for relief. Pension income paid out is generally
taxable; qualifying lump sums can be tax-free within conditions and limits.

Nuances: personal contribution relief has an earnings-related limit; carry-forward
may help with the annual allowance, while tapering and the money purchase annual
allowance can restrict the result. Details:
[member relief](pensions-tax-manual/ptm044000.txt),
[annual allowance](pensions-tax-manual/ptm050000.txt),
[lump sums](pensions-tax-manual/ptm170001.txt),
[pension income](employment-income-manual/eim75000.txt),
[saved contribution explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/tax-on-your-private-pension/index.txt).

## 10. National Insurance: a separate contribution calculation

Employees generally pay Class 1 contributions on relevant earnings through payroll;
employers have separate liabilities. Self-employed people generally pay Class 4
on relevant profits above the applicable threshold. Ordinary investment income
does not automatically attract employment or trading contributions.

For 2025–26, eligible self-employed people at or above the small-profits threshold
are treated as having paid Class 2 without actually paying it. Below that threshold,
voluntary Class 2 may protect their contribution record. Class 3 provides another
voluntary route in eligible cases. The record can affect State Pension and
certain benefit entitlements.

Nuances: age, overseas work, overlapping employment/self-employment and special
occupations can alter liability. Details:
[Class 1](national-insurance-manual/nim01000co.txt),
[Class 4](national-insurance-manual/nim24000co.txt),
[Class 2](national-insurance-manual/nim70000.txt),
[special cases](national-insurance-manual/nim27000co.txt),
[saved self-employed explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/self-employed-national-insurance-rates/index.txt).

## 11. Residence and overseas income: establish the UK's taxing scope

UK residents are generally taxed on worldwide income and gains; non-residents
generally face UK tax on relevant UK-source income and specified UK gains.
Residence follows statutory tests, not nationality or a bank's location.
Treaties and foreign-tax-credit rules may relieve double taxation, but a credit
is limited and does not necessarily refund all foreign tax.

From 6 April 2025, qualifying new residents can claim relief under the four-year
foreign income and gains regime after the required ten-year period of non-residence.
It covers eligible foreign sources, not all income automatically. A claim loses
personal allowances, including the Personal Allowance and capital-gains annual
exemption. Foreign employment earnings require separate treatment.

Nuances: split years, temporary non-residence and treaty residence can change the
answer. Pre-2025 untaxed foreign amounts can still raise remittance questions.
Details: [residence tests](residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig20000.txt),
[claim effects](residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig43000.txt),
[temporary repatriation](residence-domicile-and-remittance-basis/rdrm70000.txt),
[saved foreign-income explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/tax-foreign-income/index.txt),
[saved four-year regime](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/guidance__check-if-you-can-claim-the-4-year-foreign-income-and-gains-regime/index.txt).

## 12. Trusts and estates: identify who is taxable

Trusts do not all share one tax treatment. With a bare trust, income and gains
are generally attributed to the beneficiary. Other trusts can involve trustee-level
tax and beneficiary reporting, with payments carrying tax credits. Settlements
rules can instead attribute income to the person who provided the assets.

An estate can earn taxable income during administration. Receiving estate income
is different from inheriting capital. The trust type and distribution statement
matter: a bank receipt alone does not establish taxable income or its tax credit.

Nuances: discretionary and interest-in-possession trusts differ; non-resident trusts
and settlor interests require specialist checks. Jointly held income also follows
ownership rules, not an arbitrary chosen split. Details:
[trust income](trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem3000.txt),
[settlements](trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem4000.txt),
[ownership](trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9000.txt),
[saved trust explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/trusts-taxes/index.txt).

## 13. Inheritance Tax: transfers of wealth, not annual earnings

Inheritance Tax can apply to an estate on death and certain lifetime transfers.
Broadly, relevant asset values less allowable liabilities are reduced by
exemptions, reliefs and available nil-rate bands before the taxable remainder
is charged. Personal representatives generally deal with estate tax; inherited
capital is not ordinary earnings of the recipient.

Many outright gifts to individuals fall outside the charge if the donor survives
seven years, but that is not a universal exemption for every transfer.
Keeping a benefit from gifted property can keep it relevant to the estate.
Transfers involving trusts can have different charges.

Nuances: spouse/civil-partner and charity exemptions, residence-related bands,
business/agricultural relief and overseas assets depend on conditions and dates.
Details: [lifetime transfers](inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14000.txt),
[exemptions](inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11000.txt),
[business relief](inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25000.txt),
[long-term residence](inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm47000.txt),
[saved inheritance explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/inheritance-tax/index.txt).

## 14. Self Assessment: reconcile liabilities and payments

Self Assessment is not an extra tax. It reports income, gains, reliefs and charges
so annual liabilities can be calculated. Withheld tax and advance payments are
credited to establish the balance. Overpayment can produce a refund.

Payments on account are advance instalments towards a later year, usually based
on the preceding year's relevant liability. A payment deadline can include both
a balancing payment for the completed year and an advance instalment for the next.
Capital Gains Tax is not normally part of the payments-on-account calculation.

Nuances: reducing advances too far can lead to interest; filing and payment are
separate obligations; amendments and separate disposal reporting can matter.
Details: [advance payments](self-assessment-manual/sam1000.txt),
[amendments](self-assessment-manual/sam124000.txt),
[penalties](self-assessment-manual/sam61000.txt),
[saved bill explanation](../tax-explainers/20260919T080223011317Z/guides/understand-self-assessment-bill/index.txt).
