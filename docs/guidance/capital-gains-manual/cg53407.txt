The debt on a security is a chargeable asset. TCGA92/S251 (1) refers to ‘the debt on a security’. Section 252(2) refers to ‘a debt on a security’. The distinction is not important. In Aberdeen Construction Group Ltd v CIR 52TC281 Lord Russell said "I do not know why 'the debt'. I take the phrase simply to mean `a debt' the language used in [sub para] (2)." Advice on how to establish whether a debt is the debt on a security is at CG53420 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg53420)+.

In addition, some debts are specifically treated as securities for capital gains purposes. See CG53440 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg53440)+.

Many debts on a security will be qualifying corporate bonds (QCBs). QCBs are themselves exempt from Capital Gains Tax, though gains or losses from earlier events can crystallise on the disposal of a QCB. Detailed advice on QCBs is at CG53700 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg53700)+.
