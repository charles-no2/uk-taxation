TCGA 92/Sch 8/Para 3

A landlord may receive a capital sum from a tenant in return for the variation of one or more of the terms of a lease or for the commutation of the rent due under the lease. The way in which the gain accruing to the landlord is calculated depends principally on three factors:

- whether the payment was made in commutation of the rent due under the lease, TCGA92/Sch 8/Para 3 (2), or whether it was made for the variation or waiver of one or more of the terms of the lease, TCGA92/Sch 8/Para 3 (3); and

- if the payment was made in commutation of the rent due, whether the terms of the lease provided for such a commutation payment; and

- whether the payment was made on or after 6 April 1996.

The main circumstances which will be encountered, and their treatments, can be summarised as follows:

-

If the payment was made for the waiver or variation of one or more of the terms of the lease, the capital sum is treated as a premium, or additional premium. The instructions at CG71352 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71352)-CG71371 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71371) deal with this type of case.

-

If the payment was made in commutation of the rent due under the lease and the terms of the lease provided for the possibility of such a commutation payment, the capital sum is treated as a premium, or additional premium. Again the instructions at CG71352 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71352)-CG71371 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71371) deal with this type of case.

-

If the payment was made in commutation of the rent due under the lease and the terms of the lease did not provide for such a payment, the capital sum is chargeable under TCGA92/S22 (1). In such a case, the date of disposal is determined by the normal rules, see CG14250+ (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg14250p), and the gain or loss arising is calculated in the same way as that arising on any other part disposal. The example at CG71372 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71372) illustrates how this type of case is dealt with in practice.
