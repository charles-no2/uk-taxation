Leases: Disposal: Introduction

There are two basic methods by which a disposal of a lease can take place:

- assignment,

- surrender.

Where a lease is assigned, the tenant passes all the benefits and obligations of the lease to the new tenant (‘the assignee’).

Where a lease is surrendered, it is passed back to the landlord and will normally merge with the landlord’s superior interest (that is, the landlord’s freehold or superior leasehold interest).

Whether a lease is assigned or surrendered, the tenant will have disposed of his entire interest in the lease. The guidance at CG71120 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71120) and CG71140P (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71140p) applies equally to assignments and surrenders. The guidance at CG71210 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71210) only applies to an assignment of a lease and the guidance at CG71230P (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71230p) only applies to the surrender of a lease.

A part-disposal of a lease will occur if the tenant grants a sub-lease of the whole or part of the property covered by the lease. This is a part-disposal because the tenant will have retained a part of his original interest in the property. The guidance on dealing with such cases is at CG70950P (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70950p) and CG71000P (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71000p).

There will also be a disposal or part-disposal of the lease if a capital sum is derived from it, TCGA92/S22.

If the parties to the transaction are connected, see CG14560 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg14560) onwards, or the transaction is not at arms length, the disposal will be deemed to have taken place at market value, see CG14530 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg14530) onwards.
