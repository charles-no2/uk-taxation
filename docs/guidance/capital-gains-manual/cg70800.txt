Leases: granting of a lease: introduction

This section deals with the Capital Gains Tax consequences of the granting of a lease. As explained in CG70770 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70770), the granting of a lease is the part-disposal of the freehold or leasehold interest held by the grantor.

A chargeable gain or allowable loss will arise on the grant of a lease. There are three basic situations which are commonly met:

- where a premium is paid;

- where no premium is paid because the rent charged under the lease is a rack rent, see CG70751 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70751);

- where the lease is granted otherwise than at arms length.

Each of these situations is covered in the guidance in this section.

The implications of a reverse premium are also considered.

The guidance in this section is sub-divided as follows:

- premiums and reverse premiums, see CG70820 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70820) onwards

- reverse premiums, see CG70830 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70830)

- interaction with the charge as property income, see CG70900P (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70900p)

- long and short leases granted out of freeholds or long leases, see CG70950P (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70950p)

- short leases granted out of short leases, see CG71000P (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg71000p)

- capital improvements by the tenant, see CG70840 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg70840)
