Development value: introduction

The value of land often increases dramatically when it acquires development value, for example following its adoption within a Local Authority development plan or following the granting of planning permission. This fact has given rise to numerous special rules, and indeed special taxes. The main examples are as follows:-

- For land held at 6 April 1965, and disposed of with development value, time- apportionment (see CG15500 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg15500)) does not apply; see CG72602 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg72602).

- For disposals between 6 April 1967 and 22 July 1970, Betterment Levy, see CG72650 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg72650), was chargeable; the general guidance on this has now been archived.

- For disposals between 18 December 1973 and 31 July 1976, there was a special charge on development gains; the guidance on this has now been archived.

- For disposals between 1 August 1976 and 18 March 1985, Development Land Tax, see CG72660 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg72660), was chargeable; the general guidance on this has now been archived.

The only special rule which is still of general relevance is the mandatory 6 April 1965 valuation rule where rebasing to March 1982 is not required (mainly for companies who have not made a rebasing election), although where Betterment Levy or Development Land Tax has been paid on a previous occasion, special reliefs may be available, see CG72650 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg72650) or CG72660 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg72660) respectively.
