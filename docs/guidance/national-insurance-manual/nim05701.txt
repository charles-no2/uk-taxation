Class 1 NICs: Expenses and Allowances: Mileage allowances: Introduction

In broad terms, mileage allowances fall into two main categories:

- payments aimed at covering a range of expenses connected with motoring. These are normally paid to those employees who are required to use a privately owned car for business travel, see NIM05702 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim05702)

- payments only for fuel. Employers may pay these to employees who use vehicles the employer provides, see NIM16175 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim16175); or they may pay them to employees who use a privately owned car for business travel, see:

- NIM05708 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim05708) for payments made before 6 April 2002; and

- NIM05800 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim05800) for payments made after 5 April 2002.

Where, unusually, an employer pays a mileage allowance to an employee wholly for private travel, see:

- NIM05708 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim05708) where the car is privately owned for payments made before 6 April 2002

- NIM05800 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim05800) where the car is privately owned for payments made after 5 April 2002

- NIM16175 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim16175) where the employer provides an employee with a car.

The definitions and rules applied to determine qualifying business travel, including business mileage, are the same for both tax and NICs, see EIM32015 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32015).
