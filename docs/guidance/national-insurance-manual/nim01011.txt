Earning limits/thresholds

Regulation 10 of The Social Security (Contributions) Regulations (SSCR) 2001 (SI 2001 No 1004)

There is a Lower Earnings Limit (‘LEL’, see NIM01005 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01005)) and a Primary and a Secondary Threshold (see NIM01008 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01008)). No primary contributions are paid on earnings between the LEL and the Primary Threshold, with NICs becoming payable only when earnings exceed the Primary Threshold. However, primary contributions (see NIM01001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01001)) are treated as having been paid on earnings at the LEL (see NIM01005 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01005)) up to and including the Primary Threshold (see also NIM01007 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01007) ). Secondary contributions are payable once earnings exceed the Secondary Threshold (see NIM01001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01001)), which from 6 April 2025 is set at lower amount than the LEL.

There is also an Upper Earnings Limit (UEL). Primary Class 1 NICs are payable at the standard percentage rate on earnings up to the UEL and at a lower percentage rate on earnings above (see NIM01009 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01009)). Secondary Class 1 NICs are payable at the standard percentage rate on all earnings above the UEL.

For guidance on the structure of Class 1 NICs from 6 April 2003 (see NIM01100 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01100)).

For guidance on the structure of Class 1 NICs from 6 April 2009 (see NIM01200 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01200)).

For further guidance on calculating and recording NICs, including NICs rates, see NIM11000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim11000) onwards. If aggregation of earnings is involved (NIM01004 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01004)), see also NIM10000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim10000) onwards.

NICs rebate

Up to 5 April 2016, when contracting-out ceased, some employers operated contracted-out pension schemes, where they and their employees who were members of the schemes receive a reduction in their NICs. The reduction, for both employees and employers, was realised via a reduction in the NICs percentage rate applied to earnings between the LEL and the UEL. The percentage rate differed for employees and employers and for the different types of contracted-out schemes. The difference between the full contracted-out rate and these reduced rates is known as the 'contracted-out rebate'.

For further information about contracting-out see NIM01017 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01017). For further guidance on calculating and recording the contracted-out rebate, including rebated rates, see NIM11000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim11000) onwards. If aggregation of earnings is involved (see NIM01004 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01004)), see also NIM10000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim10000) onwards.
