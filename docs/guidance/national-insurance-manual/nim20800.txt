Class 2 National Insurance contributions liability: Small earnings from subsidiary self-employment: Administrative concession
Identifier: NIM20800
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim20800
Published: 2016-04-11T17:37:43+01:00
Updated: 2022-03-09T13:42:37+00:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T15:15:56.146954+00:00

Previously, a person who was in concurrent Class 1 and Class 2 employment did not need to pay Class 2 contributions or apply for SEE (NIM21000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim21000)) if -

- Their earnings from Class 1 employment were at or above the Lower Earnings Limit (LEL - See NIM01005 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01005)); and

- Their net earnings from self-employment were expected to be £1,300 or less in the tax year.

This was a concession and was not written into legislation.

Following the House of Lord’s decision in R v IRC, ex parte Wilkinson [2005] UKHL 30, HMRC reviewed administrative concessions and this one was removed.

Any person who was self-employed had to register as such and if they had low income from self-employment they had to claim SEE (see NIM21000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim21000)) if they did not want to pay Class 2 contributions.
