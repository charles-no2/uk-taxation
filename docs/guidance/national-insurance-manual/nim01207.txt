Regulation 10 of the Social Security Contributions Regulations 2001 (SSCR 2001)

Earnings limits and Thresholds

From 6 April 2009 there is still a

- Lower Earnings Limit (LEL), see NIM01005 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01005),

- Secondary Threshold (ST), see NIM01008 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01008) and

- Primary Threshold (PT), see NIM01008 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01008).

No contributions are paid on earnings between the LEL and the Primary Threshold, with NICs becoming payable only when earnings exceed the Primary Threshold. However, primary contributions, see NIM01001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01001), are treated as having been paid on earnings at the LEL, see NIM01005 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01005), up to and including the Primary Threshold (see also NIM01007 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01007) covering ‘Notional Primary’). Secondary contributions become payable once earnings exceed the Secondary Threshold, see NIM01001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01001).

There is still an Upper Earnings Limit (UEL). This is the maximum amount of earnings on which an earner pays NICs at the Class 1 main primary percentage.

NIC rebate

From 6 April 2009 to 5 April 2016 there was an Upper Accrual Point (UAP), see NIM01205 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01205). This was the maximum amount of earnings on which an earner who is contracted out of the state pension scheme can receive a NICs rebate.

Where employers operate contracted-out pension schemes they and their employees who are members of the schemes receive a reduction in their NICs. The reduction, for both employees and employers, is in effect realised via a reduction in the NICs percentage rate applied to earnings between the LEL and the UAP. The percentage rate differs for employees and employers. The difference between the full contracted-out rate and these reduced rates is known as the contracted-out rebate’.

From 6 April 2012 the NIC rebate for employees was reduced from 1.6% to 1.4% and for employers from 3.7% to 3.4%

From 6 April 2016 Contracting-out was abolished and this page no longer applies to tax years from this date, see NIM01209 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01209).

For further information about

- Contracting-out, see NIM01017 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim01017).

- Calculating and recording the contracted-out rebate, including rebated rates, see NIM11000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim11000) onwards.
