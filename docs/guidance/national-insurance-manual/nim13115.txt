Section 10(2)(b) of Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992 (SSCBA 1992)

Background

NIM13101  (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim13101)explains that the person liable to pay secondary (employer) Class 1 NICs is the person liable to pay Class 1A NICs on the last or only ’relevant payment of earnings’ to an earner in the tax year in which the benefit is provided.

NIM13111  (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim13111)explains what is meant by a relevant payments of earnings.

No earnings on which Class 1 NICs are due

Where no earnings are paid which would attract Class 1 NICs liability, but a benefit in kind on which Class 1A NICs would be due is provided, the person liable to pay Class 1A NICs is the person who would be liable to pay Class 1 NICs if the benefit was subject to Class 1 NICs. This does not mean that the benefit attracts a Class 1 NICs liability. The benefit is simply treated as a payment of earnings for the purposes of identifying a secondary contributor liable to pay Class 1A NICs.

See NIM13080 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim13080) for information on excluded employments which applied for tax years up to 2015 to 2016.

Example

A director is voted dividends by his company. The company also reimburses the director the cost of business calls made from his home telephone and his qualifying travel expenses. The director receives no other earnings but is provided with a car which is used for both business and private purposes.

While no Class 1 NICs liability arises on either the dividends or the payment of the director’s business expenses, the car is treated as a relevant payment of earnings. The employer is liable to pay Class 1A NICs in respect of the car.
