Loss on sale of land: disadvantageous claim
Identifier: IHTM33013
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33013
Published: 2016-04-09T10:08:04+01:00
Updated: 2020-03-16T07:31:57+00:00

A claim following a sale at less than death value may not always be to the taxpayer’s advantage because

- there are various provisions for adjusting the sale price (IHTM33072 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33072))

- the relief involves substituting the sale value (IHTM33073 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33073)) for the date of death value (IHTM33100 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33100)) for all interests in land sold (IHTM33090 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33090)) by the claimant in the same capacity

- in the case of jointly owned property, there is no joint property discount from the gross sale price of the entire property (IHTM33182 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33182))

The taxpayer cannot withdraw a claim once it is made.

The taxpayer may try to make a claim following a sale at more than the date of death value even though it is clearly disadvantageous for Inheritance Tax purposes. Where this happens you should follow the instructions at IHTM33026 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33026).
