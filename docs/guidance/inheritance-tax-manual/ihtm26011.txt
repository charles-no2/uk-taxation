Specific gifts: definition
Identifier: IHTM26011

For the purpose of the partly exempt transfer rules (IHTM26071 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26071)) a specific gift is any gift other than a gift of the residue or a share of the residue, under IHTA84/S42(1). Typical specific gifts are:

- pecuniary legacies (IHTM12082 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12082)), whether given absolutely or settled

-

gifts of particular assets such as

- the deceased’s residence

- furniture, jewellery and other household and personal goods and effects

- shares in companies and business assets.

More unusual specific gifts include

- a provision in the Will (IHTM12041 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12041)) that a person who owes the deceased money is forgiven the debt, so the debt is cancelled and no longer repayable. (Note that the debt is still an asset of the estate)

- the statutory legacy (IHTM12122 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12122)) and household and personal goods to which a spouse or civil partner (IHTM11032 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11032)) is absolutely entitled under the intestacy laws (IHTM12121 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12121)) other than in Scotland

- legal rights (IHTM12221 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12221)) under the law of Scotland (for the burden of tax see IHTM26201 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26201)).

- a provision in the Will that the Inheritance Tax on a lifetime transfer (IHTM14001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14001)) is to be paid out of the residuary estate . The person who received the gift will get a pecuniary legacy of the amount of the tax
