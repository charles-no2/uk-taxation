Transfers on death: general rule

The general rule is that the personal representatives (IHTM05012 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm05012)) are accountable. They have to complete and return an IHT400 for the deceased’s estate. The only exception to this rule is where the estate is an ‘excepted estate’ (IHTM06011 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm06011)).

The time limit for delivering the account is

- twelve months from the end of the month in which the death occurs, or

- if later, three months from the date when the personal representatives first acted in that capacity.

Penalties (IHTM36021 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm36021)) may be chargeable if the time limits are not met.

There are special rules covering

- what property (IHTM10812 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10812)) needs to be included in the account

- the provision of estimated values (IHTM10813 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10813)), and

- what happens when the personal representatives do not obtain a grant within the normal time limits (IHTM10814 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10814)).
