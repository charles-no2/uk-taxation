The settlor: limitations on settlor's liability
Identifier: IHTM30112
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm30112
Published: 2016-04-09T09:57:22+01:00
Updated: 2018-03-12T13:33:00+00:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T15:04:53.784886+00:00

The settlor may be liable for tax on

- an ending during lifetime of an interest in possession (IHTM30281 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm30281)), whether it is a potentially exempt transfer (PETs) or chargeable when made and

- charges under the relevant property trust (IHTM16042 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16042)) regime.

Under IHTA84/S201 (1)(d) the settlor is liable if two basic conditions are satisfied

- the transfer takes place during the settlor’s lifetime

- the trustees are for the time being resident outside the UK.

These two conditions are basic. If they are not satisfied, the settlor cannot be liable under IHTA84/S201 (1)(d). But if the conditions are satisfied, the settlor is not necessarily liable. IHTA84/S201 contains three subsections excluding liability under IHTA84/S201 (1)(d) in situations relating to PETs (IHTM30113 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm30113)) and where a settlement was made before 11 December 1974 (IHTM30115 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm30115)).
