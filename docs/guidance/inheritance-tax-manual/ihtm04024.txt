Structure of the charge: what is a transfer of value?
Identifier: IHTM04024
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04024
Published: 2016-03-20T09:15:11+00:00
Updated: 2018-03-27T10:48:09+01:00

A transfer of value is a disposition (IHTM04023 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04023)) made by a person as a result of which the value of their estate has decreased. All lifetime transfers must start with a disposition. Charges arising on death and on settled property where a qualifying interest in possession exists (IHTM16062 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16062)) are deemed transfers of value.

A transfer of value may only qualify as a potentially exempt transfer (IHTM04057 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04057)) to the extent that it would, apart from IHTA84/S3A, be a chargeable transfer. This means that a transfer of value may be partly an exempt transfer (IHTM04026 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04026)) and partly a potentially exempt transfer.

For example, Alison gives Belinda £10,000. As it is Alison’s first transfer of value in the tax year the annual exemption (IHTM14141 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14141)) of £3,000 is available. So the gift is

- an exempt transfer of £3,000, and as it would, apart from IHTA84/S3A, be a chargeable transfer, it is

- a potentially exempt transfer of £7,000.
