There are a number of ways in which a whole Will can be revoked:

- by subsequent Will or codicil (IHTM12072 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12072))

- by destruction (IHTM12073 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12073))

- by marriage or civil partnership (IHTM11032 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11032)) (IHTM12074 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12074)) (but this does not apply in Scotland)

A Will can be partially revoked (IHTM12075 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12075)) by divorce or dissolution of a marriage or civil partnership.

In Scotland, a Will which makes no provision for the children of the testator is presumed to be revoked by the subsequent birth of a child. The law assumes that the testator would not have wished a Will which made no provision for a later-born child to be given effect to. This applies equally where the deceased has died so soon after the birth that they had no opportunity to make a new Will, or where a considerable period of time has elapsed.
