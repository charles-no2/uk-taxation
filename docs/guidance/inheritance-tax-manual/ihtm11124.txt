Procedure: straightforward gifts and acceptable bequests
Identifier: IHTM11124
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11124
Published: 2016-03-20T09:33:16+00:00
Updated: 2026-04-07T01:00:00+01:00

A gift is straightforward if it is expressed as being given absolutely and unconditionally to a charity (IHTM11112 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11112)). Sometimes expressions of intent or wishes are attached to the legacy. This does not necessarily prevent them from being straightforward.

A gift is considered not to be straightforward where

- it is expressed to be for a special purpose (IHTM11175 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11175)), or

- it is for a subsidiary fund (IHTM11126 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11126)) of an organisation

- some element of avoidance of tax (IHTM11171 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11171)) appears to be involved

- a family or private (IHTM11130 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11130)) charity is involved

Be careful to identify the particular beneficiary involved, for example, a bequest to Amnesty International is not a gift to Amnesty International UK Section Charitable Trust. Remember that many ‘non-charitable’ organisations have a sister charity.

You should accept a gift by the deceased’s will (IHTM12041 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12041)) as being exempt (assuming the beneficiary is accepted as a charity (IHTM11112 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11112))) provided

- it is not subject to a condition (IHTM11175 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11175)) which is not satisfied within twelve months after the transfer

- it takes effect in possession immediately (IHTM11174 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11174)) on the death

- it is not defeasible (IHTM11173 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11173)) (for example, a gift which can be revoked or terminated by a future event)

- it may not become applicable for other than charitable (IHTM11172 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11172)) purposes

- it consists of either the whole (IHTM11176 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11176)) of the deceased’s interest in an asset, or the whole of some lesser interest for example, deceased’s half share of an asset

Example 1

Amy dies.

By Will she leaves the residue of her estate to Liam for life. On Liam's death it passes to a qualifying charity.

Do not accept. The gift is not exempt on Amy’s death because the gift to the charity does not take effect in possession immediately on her death.

Example 2

Amy dies owning the freehold interest in the whole of ‘The Manor’.

By Will Amy makes a gift of a half share in ‘The Manor’ to a qualifying charity absolutely.

Accept. The gift is of the whole of the deceased’s interest in the half share.

Example 3

The facts are as in Example 2.

By Will Amy makes a gift of ‘The Manor’ to a qualifying charity for 20 years.

Do not accept. The gift is not of the whole of the deceased’s interest in the property because it is for a limited period of time.
