Loss on sale of land: changes in the interest or underlying land: where revaluation is for more than the death value
Identifier: IHTM33123
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33123
Published: 2016-04-09T10:09:03+01:00
Updated: 2016-02-11T17:20:08+00:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T15:06:57.519011+00:00

Where the revaluation of the interest results in a revised date of death value (IHTM33100 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33100)) that is greater, the sale price (IHTM33072 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33072)) is reduced to what it would have been if the changes had not occurred.

Example

At the date of death the market value of a parcel of land is £50,000.

Within the four year period a house is built on the land.

The market value at the date of sale of the land with the house is £300,000.

But, without the house, the value of the land would have been £45,000

If the house had been on the land at the date of death its value would have been more than £50,000 (the value of the land at the date of death).

For relief purposes the sale value is taken to be £45,000.

You should ask the VOA (IHTM23185 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm23185)) to agree the value of the interest at the date of sale (IHTM33071 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33071)) as if the changes had not occurred and the interest remained in the same condition it was in at the date of death. This revised value is the sale value (IHTM33073 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm33073)).
