The extent of the share (England, Wales and Northern Ireland): lifetime gifts arising out of a transfer of an account into joint names
Identifier: IHTM15043
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15043
Published: 2016-04-09T09:23:13+01:00
Updated: 2023-09-22T17:01:50+01:00

If Arthur places money in a joint account (IHTM15042 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15042)) in the names of Arthur and Brenda as joint tenants (IHTM15082 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15082)) and retains the right to withdraw the whole of it, as a general rule there will not be a lifetime transfer (IHTM15060 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15060)) at the time the money is paid into the account. But if any part is subsequently withdrawn for the benefit of Brenda, the other joint owner, there may be a transfer at that time.

Refer to Technical any case where

- a withdrawal of this kind has been made

- it is claimed that there was an immediate gift when the money was paid into the joint account

- there is evidence that an immediate gift was intended, or

- the position is more complicated - for example where withdrawals need both signatures

Reference should be made to the cases quoted at IHTM15042 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15042).
