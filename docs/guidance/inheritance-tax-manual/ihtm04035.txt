Structure of the charge: what is general power  property?
Identifier: IHTM04035
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04035
Published: 2016-03-20T09:15:29+00:00

Property (IHTM04030 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04030)) brought within a person’s estate (IHTM04029 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04029)) by IHTA84/S5 (2) includes

- on that person’s death, unsettled property which he or she can dispose of by will (Att Gen v Quixley [1929] 141 LT 288)

- an absolute interest in property which is given (whether by lifetime transfer or on death) to a person, but which he (or his personal representative) disclaims. The property is included in the person’s estate on the grounds that he had a power enabling him to ‘dispose of property as he thinks fit’. This is subject to the reliefs in IHTA84/S17 (1), IHTA84/S93 and IHTA84/S142.

- some policies with certain Canadian insurance companies (IHTM20611 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm20611)).

There are two special rules for pension schemes which mean that a ‘general power’ as defined in IHTA84/S5 (2) does not apply

- to certain unexercised cash options under approved annuity schemes, (IHTM17000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm17000)) IHTA84/S152, and

- approved pension schemes (IHTM20000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm20000)) etc, even though the property concerned may be settled property, IHTA84/S151 (4).
