Step 4 - grossing up: deciding which type of grossing calculation to use

There are three different types of grossing up (IHTM26121 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26121)) calculation which are

- a simple (IHTM26141 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26141)) grossing calculation

- a four stage (IHTM26152 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26152)) calculation, and

- a ‘Benham’ (IHTM26171 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26171)) grossing calculation

The simple calculation can be used only when at the same title (IHTM26212 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26212)),

- all the chargeable specific gifts (IHTM26011 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26011)) are free of tax (IHTM26123 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26123)), and

- the residue (IHTM26003 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26003)) is wholly exempt

The four stage calculation must be used where, at the same title, there are

- chargeable specific gifts which are free of tax, and chargeable specific gifts bearing their own tax and/or

- the residue is wholly or partly chargeable (though, as mentioned in IHTM26151 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26151) grossing up is rarely necessary if the residue is wholly chargeable)

‘Benham’ type grossing is very rare and occurs where a testator provides for the residuary beneficiaries to receive specified proportions after all the Inheritance Tax has been paid.
