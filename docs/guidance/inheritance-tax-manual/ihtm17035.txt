Pensions: IHT exclusions: contributions to a pension scheme
Identifier: IHTM17035
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm17035

Updated: 2016-02-11T17:05:21+00:00

Generally, when a person makes a contribution to a pension scheme, they do not make a transfer of value as the pension rights they acquire by making the contribution are within their estate. But, there are exceptions - IHTM17042 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm17042) and IHTM17043 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm17043) describe specific situations where contributions can be transfers of value.

There is also a specific exclusion in IHTA1984/S12(2) where a disposition is made by an employer. The disposition is not a transfer of value if it is:

- a contribution under a registered pension scheme (IHTM17021 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm17021)),

- a qualifying non-UK pension scheme (IHTM17025 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm17025)) or

- a section 615(3) scheme (IHTM17026 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm17026)) in respect of an employee of the person making the disposition.
