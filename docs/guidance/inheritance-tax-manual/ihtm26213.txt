As well as the example below further examples may be found in the instructions on interaction (IHTM26101 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26101)) and grossing up (IHTM26121 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26121)). For an example including both interaction and grossing refer to IHTM26158 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26158).

Example

Tanya died in August 2002 leaving an estate of £500,000. By Will Tanya left a legacy of £300,000 free of tax to Xavier, a chargeable beneficiary, and the rest of the estate to a qualifying charity. There is no business relief (BR) (IHTM25131 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25131)) or agricultural relief (AR) (IHTM24001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm24001)).

Tanya was also life tenant of settled property (IHTM16000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16000)) valued at £2M. The trust property includes agricultural property qualifying for AR. The trust passes to a chargeable beneficiary.

The effect of S40 on the calculation of Xavier’s £300,000 legacy is:

- There is no interaction because there is no BR or AR in the free estate and the AR on the settled property is irrelevant.

- The £300,000 is grossed up at its own rate and, for grossing purposes, the settled property is ignored.

The grossing calculation is:

Legacy = £300,000

Less nil-rate band -£250,000

Excess = £50,000

Excess of £50,000 × (100 ÷ 60) = £83,333

Add back nil-rate band +£250,000

Grossed-up value of legacy = £333,333

The grossed up value of the £300,000 legacy, and consequently the chargeable estate on Tanya’s death, is £333,333 plus the value of the settled fund after AR. Tax is assessed accordingly.
