Types of joint property: introduction
Identifier: IHTM15081

There are two ways that property in the UK can be jointly owned. These are where:

- the share of the first to die passes automatically to the other joint owners. Where this applies the share is said to pass ‘by survivorship’, or

- each joint owner may give their share to someone else under their Will (IHTM12041 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12041)) (or if they did not make a will, under the rules of intestacy (IHTM12101 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12101))).

Although these two principles apply throughout the UK, there are differences between Scottish (IHTM15091 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15091)) law and the law for the rest of the UK (IHTM15082 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15082)).
