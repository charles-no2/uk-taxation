IHT implications of an Instrument of Variation: effect of coming within s.142
Identifier: IHTM35151
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm35151
Published: 2016-04-09T10:14:24+01:00
Updated: 2017-09-13T12:53:38+01:00

When a variation satisfies the requirements of IHTA84/S142(1) and there is a valid election or, on or after 1 August 2002, a valid statement of intent

- the variation is not a transfer of value, and

- the IHTA84 applies as if the deceased had effected the variation

Consequently, for example

- if the extent to which the death estate is exempt is increased (IHTM35152 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm35152)) or decreased, you may need to adjust the tax (IHTM35153 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm35153)) on the whole of the death estate, including property taxable at a separate title, such as settled property in which the deceased had a life interest,

- if a variation sets up a non-interest in possession trust, the deceased is treated as the settlor, and

- the gift with reservation rules in FA86/S102 cannot apply to a disposition which is accepted as a variation within IHTA84/S142(1). This is because the effect of IHTA84/S142(1) is that the deceased is treated as the donor.
