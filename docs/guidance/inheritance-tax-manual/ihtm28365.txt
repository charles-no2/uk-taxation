Liabilities: investigating form IHT419: how FA86/S103 applies when the consideration is ‘property derived from the deceased’?
Identifier: IHTM28365
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28365
Published: 2016-04-09T09:54:56+01:00
Updated: 2016-02-11T17:14:34+00:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T15:04:26.931692+00:00

The legislation disallows a debt to the extent that the consideration for it consisted of property derived from the deceased, FA86/S103 (1)(a). ‘Property derived from the deceased’ (IHTM28367 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28367)) is defined in FA86/S103 (3).

This provision would defeat the following arrangement:

Example

- On 19 March 1987 Adam gives his brother Brian £25,000.

- On 25 April 1987 Adam borrows back £25,000 from Brian.

- On 7 April 1994 Adam dies.

Without the legislation Adam’s estate includes the original £25,000. But if the money was still owed when Adam died the debt might be claimed as a deduction against his estate. And the PET in 1987 is exempt as more than 7 years have elapsed.

The legislation disallows the deduction for Inheritance Tax purposes. The debt is treated as a specific gift so grossing (IHTM26000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26000)) may be required. If the deceased had repaid some of the loan any repayments made within 7 years of death would be treated as PETs (IHTM04057 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04057)).
