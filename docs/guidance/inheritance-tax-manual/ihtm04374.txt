Woodlands: the death estate

For the relief to be available, the trees or underwood must be growing on land which is reflected in the value of a person’s estate (IHTM04030 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04030)) immediately before his death - IHTA84/S125 (1)(a). The normal rules apply to determine what constitutes a deceased’s death estate for this purpose. The main categories included and so for which the relief may be available (the other conditions also have to be satisfied) are

- the free estate

- the deceased’s severable share of joint property, (IHTM15082 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15082)) or in Scotland heritable property passing under a survivorship destination (IHTM15091 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15091))

- settled property in which the deceased had a qualifying interest in possession, (I (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16000)HTM16062 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16062)) and

- property chargeable under FA86/S102 (3) as a gift with reservation of benefit (GWR) (IHTM14301 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14301)). You should refer any claim for relief where tax is chargeable under FA1984/S102 (3) to Technical to consider the question of who is beneficially entitled to GWR property under IHTA84/S125 (1)(b).

The main categories excluded from the death estate, and so for which there can be no relief, are

- lifetime transfers other than GWRs - the fact that a potentially exempt transfer (IHTM04057 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04057)) becomes chargeable solely by reason of the transferor’s death within seven years does not make it part of the death estate,

- charges on trusts under the relevant property regime,

- transfers by close companies.
