Investigation of form IHT404: general investigation points
Identifier: IHTM15023
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15023
Published: 2016-04-09T09:23:02+01:00
Updated: 2016-02-11T17:03:57+00:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T14:58:34.213504+00:00

When you review the form IHT404 you should:

- Check that the deceased’s share offered on the IHT404 matches the extent of the share (IHTM15031 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15031)) (as confirmed if necessary by Technical)

- Examine the assets in the normal (IHTM09000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm09000)) way, taking up any valuation points.

- Make sure that the deceased’s share is carried forward and shown correctly in the IHT400 (IHTM10021 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10021)) and check that any reliefs and exemptions deducted apply.

- Check that any tax on the joint property has been correctly calculated and paid

- Be on the look out for joint property in the IHT400 which is not mentioned on the IHT404, particularly where the joint property passes by Will (IHTM12041 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12041)) or intestacy (IHTM12101 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12101)). If you discover that jointly owned land on form IHT405 has already been referred to the District Valuer (IHTM23002 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm23002)) before you have considered the extent of the deceased’s share you may need to change the valuation instructions (IHTM23031 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm23031)).

You should concentrate your effort on cases where there are strong grounds for believing there will be a worthwhile value increase. Investigate any case where there are reasonable grounds for thinking that the deceased’s beneficial interest (IHTM15011 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15011)) may be different to the share offered in the IHT400 or that the arrangement may have involved a lifetime transfer (IHTM15060 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15060)) by the deceased. Also bear in mind that the rules relating to gifts with reservation (IHTM15061 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15061)) may be relevant, especially where the deceased had (or is claimed to have) made a gift to the other joint owner(s) when the joint ownership began after 18 March 1986.
