When someone dies, they leave material possessions which may amount to either a great deal or very little. Whatever the value all individuals have the right to dispose of their possessions, and to make their intentions clear everyone can make a Will (IHTM12041 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12041)). A person who makes a Will is called a testator (if they are male) or testatrix (if they are female). When a person dies without making a Will, they die intestate (IHTM12101 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12101)) and an intestacy arises.

This section of guidance deals with the formalities of making a Will, the consequences of Wills and intestacies and the practice and processes used to investigate them.

This section also considers the special rules (IHTM12201 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm12201)) that apply in Scotland known as Prior and Legal rights and includes rights under S131 of the Civil Partnership Act 2004 (IHTM11032 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11032)).
