Lifetime transfers: deemed transfers that are  potentially exempt transfers

Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04063
Published: 2016-03-20T09:15:59+00:00
Updated: 2018-04-10T17:31:56+01:00

There is one deemed transfer of value (IHTM04025 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04025)) that is a potentially exempt transfer (PET). (IHTM04057 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04057)) This is the deemed transfer under IHTA84/S52, but only if the transfer was on or after 17 March 1987. The requirement that the transfer is by an individual (IHTM04053 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04053)) is met by IHTA84/S3 (4) and IHTA84/S3A (7). The other two conditions must still be met to for a release of a life interest (IHTM16000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16000)) to qualify as a PET.

This means that a release of a life interest between 18 March 1986 and 16 March 1987 cannot be a PET and is therefore an immediately chargeable transfer. (IHTM04067 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04067)) This is because as originally enacted IHTA84/S3A (2) prevented a gift from being a PET if it resulted in

- settled property (IHTM16000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16000)) becoming comprised in an individual’s estate (IHTM04029 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04029)) or

- an increase in the value of settled property already comprised in it.

An amendment contained in F(No 2)A 87/S92 removed this restriction so that a release of life interest could qualify as a PET.
