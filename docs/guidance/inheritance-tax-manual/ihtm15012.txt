Joint property: joint property and inheritance tax

The Inheritance Tax Act contains

- no provisions that apply specifically or exclusively to lifetime charges (IHTM15060 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15060)) on joint property (IHTM15011 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15011)): and

- only one provision, IHTA84/S171 (2), that specifically relates to the charge on death. And that provision is required only to exclude the operation of IHTA84/S171 (1), (IHTM04046 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04046)).

So, any charge to tax on joint property has to be based on applying the ordinary provisions of the legislation to the beneficial entitlements (IHTM15011 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15011)). The main provisions that apply are:

- IHTA84/S3, which defines ‘transfer of value’ (IHTM04024 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04024))

- IHTA84/S4, for the charge on death (IHTM04041 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04041))

- IHTA84/S5, which defines ‘estate’ (IHTM04029 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04029)) and

- IHTA84/S272 under which ‘property’ (IHTM04030 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04030)) includes rights and interests of every description

Under these provision’s a person’s beneficial entitlement to joint property forms part of their estate for the purposes of the Inheritance Tax charge both on transfers on death and lifetime transfers.

In addition, FA 1986/S102 and FA 1999/S104 govern any gift with reservation (IHTM15061 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15061)) charge on joint property.
