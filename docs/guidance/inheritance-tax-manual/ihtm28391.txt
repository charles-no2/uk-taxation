Liabilities: law relating to debts: general rules for deducting liabilities against property
Identifier: IHTM28391
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28391
Published: 2016-04-09T09:55:17+01:00
Updated: 2016-02-11T17:14:38+00:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T15:04:30.898262+00:00

In general, debts are deducted against the value of the assets in the deceased’s free estate that do not qualify for the instalment option. The main exceptions are where there are:

- charges (IHTM28392 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28392)) on any property, such as a mortgage on land, or security is fixed on moveable (personal) property, IHTA84/S162 (4)

- business debts (IHTM28393 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28393)), or

- foreign debts (IHTM28394 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28394))

You should also take special care where there is excluded property (IHTM28395 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28395)) or where there are UK debts and there is both foreign and UK property (IHTM28396 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28396)) in the estate.

A liability at any title may only be deducted against the assets at that title. This has implications for carrying forward deficits (IHTM28397 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28397)) in one part of the estate against another.
