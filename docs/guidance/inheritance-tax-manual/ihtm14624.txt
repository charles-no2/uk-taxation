Lifetime transfers: specific lifetime reliefs: fall in value relief: conditions for relief
Identifier: IHTM14624
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14624
Published: 2016-04-09T09:20:37+01:00
Updated: 2016-02-11T17:03:29+00:00
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-09-18T14:58:06.063332+00:00

All the following conditions must be satisfied before you can allow any relief

-

The value transferred by the failed potentially exempt transfer (IHTM04057 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04057)) or chargeable transfer must be wholly or partly attributable to property which either

- remains the property of the transferee (or of the spouse or civil partner (IHTM11032 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11032)) of the transferee) at the date of death, or

- has been sold by the transferee (or spouse or civil partner of the transferee) by an earlier qualifying sale (IHTM14625 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14625)).

- The market value (IHTM14626 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14626)) of the transferred property at the time of the transfer must exceed its market value at the date of death or sale.

- A claim in writing must be made by a person liable to pay the tax, or additional tax (IHTM14571 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14571)), within 4 years of the date of the donor’s death, IHTA/S131(2ZA).
