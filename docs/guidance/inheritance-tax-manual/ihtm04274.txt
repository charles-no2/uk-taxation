Foreign settled property: identifying settled property
Identifier: IHTM04274
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04274
Published: 2016-03-20T09:18:20+00:00
Updated: 2025-04-07T01:00:00+01:00

The expression ‘property comprised in a settlement’ in IHTA84/S48 (3) means the items of property (IHTM04030 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04030)) held in the settlement (IHTM16042 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16042)) at the time of the chargeable event you are considering. In determining the locality (IHTM27071 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm27071)) of any particular property you should consider the property in its current form and not its previous history.

Example

Stella, who is never a long-term UK resident (IHTM47000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm47000#)), transfers a house in Germany and some UK securities into a settlement for Xavier (also not a long-term UK resident) for his life with remainder to Yolanda. On Xavier’s death - the potentially chargeable event - the settled fund consists of:

-

Option 1: a villa in Spain.

-

Option 2: land in the UK.

-

Option 3: a house in Spain and some UK securities.

With Option 1 the villa is excluded property even though it partly represents the proceeds of what was previously UK property (the securities).

The land in Option 2 is not excluded property although it is partly derived from the proceeds of sale of the German house.

In Option 3 the house in Spain is excluded property but the UK securities are not.
