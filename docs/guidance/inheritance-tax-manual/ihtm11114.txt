General outline: quantifying the exemption

When considering the extent of any exemption you should look out for situations where

- lifetime transfers (IHTM11013 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11013)) are involved

- the transfer is only partly exempt (IHTM26001 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm26001))

- the deceased is domiciled outside the UK (IHTM11013 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11013))

- there is a claim (IHTM11025 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11025)) against a deceased person’s estate which was settled by an exempt beneficiary (IHTM11026 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11026)) out of their own resources

- the exemption has a value limit. Inheritance Tax has no value limit. However, until 1983 there was a value limit on the corresponding exemption for Capital Transfer Tax. The limit was removed by F(No.2)A 1983. If you need to consider a transfer made before 15 March 1983 seek advice from Technical.
