The extent of the share (Scotland): land

The title to heritage is proof of its ownership, and the owners interests in it - unless there is evidence to the contrary, normally by way of written document.

If there is no special destination (IHTM15050 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15050)) and there is equal provision of the price, each co-owner can dispose of his own share as part of his estate and there is no accretion among them.

If spouses or civil partners (IHTM11032 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11032)) are the joint owners you should keep the ‘related property’ (IHTM09731 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm09731)) provisions in view (IHTA84/S161).

If it is claimed the beneficial interests (IHTM15011 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15011)) vary from those indicated by the title and the absence of gift is claimed, strong proof is required of the parties intentions such as a contemporaneous writing.
