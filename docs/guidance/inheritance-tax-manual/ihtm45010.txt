Reduced rate for gifts to charity: calculating the baseline amount: estate with a single estate component

Most estates will contain just the deceased’s free estate which is part of the general component (IHTM45007 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm45007)). In these cases, establishing the donated amount (IHTM45002 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm45002)) and the baseline amount (IHTM45009 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm45009)) will be straightforward.

Example

Robert died on 17 June 2012 leaving an estate valued at £750,000 after deduction of liabilities. He leaves a legacy of £50,000 to the National Trust.

The donated amount is £50,000. The baseline amount is calculated following the steps in IHTA/Sch1A/Para 5 as follows:

Estate on death = £750,000

Legacy to charity (donated amount) -£50,000

Chargeable transfer = £700,000 (step 1)

Less nil-rate band -£325,000

Step 2 amount = £375,000

Add back legacy to charity +£50,000

Baseline amount = £425,000 (step 3)

The charitable giving condition (IHTM45002 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm45002)) or 10% test requires that the donated amount is at least 10% of the baseline amount. Here the legacy of £50,000 is more than 10% of £425,000 (or £42,500) so the estate qualifies for the reduced rate. The reduced tax liability will be £135,000 compared to £150,000 at full rate.
