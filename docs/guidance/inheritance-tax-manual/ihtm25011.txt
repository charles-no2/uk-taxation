Investigating business relief and businesses: Introduction

You should start your investigation by looking at the information on form IHT413 or form D38 (IHTM25012 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25012)), then check the instructions on 100% relief cases (IHTM25021 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25021)) to see if 100% business relief applies.

In your investigation you should review all the information that is available to you. This will include the

- deceased’s/transferor’s stated occupation,

- information provided on form IHT 412, IHT413, IHT437 or form D38, (IHTM25012 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25012))

- information provided about the deceased’s/transferor’s business activities,

- information provided in any business accounts (IHTM25052 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25052)),

- terms of any partnership deeds (IHTM25092 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25092)) that may exist, and

- information in other HMRC files such as Income Tax or company files, other Inheritance Tax files for the taxpayer, for example a lifetime file or for other members of the family/ business.

All this information will help you to establish whether the deceased/transferor meets the conditions to qualify for business relief and, if so, the rate of relief and the extent to which the value transferred (IHTM04028 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04028)) is entitled to relief.

If relief is not due you should review all the available information to establish the value of the business interest.
