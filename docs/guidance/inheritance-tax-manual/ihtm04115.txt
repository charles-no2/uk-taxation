Heritage property: chargeable events under  IHTA84/S32A
Identifier: IHTM04115
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04115
Published: 2016-03-20T09:17:03+00:00
Updated: 2018-04-18T16:31:47+01:00

The events that cause a charge to tax on a conditionally exempt transfer of properties that are associated with each other are

- failure to observe the undertaking given to secure conditional exemption, IHTA84/S32A (3),

- death of the person beneficially entitled (IHTM04031 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04031)) to the assets, IHTA84/S32A (4)(a), or

- disposal of the assets, by sale, gift or otherwise, IHTA84/S32A (4)(b).

However, you should note that though the event may apply only to one of the associated properties, the charge arising under IHTA84/S32A will apply to the all of the properties that are associated with each other.

There are a number of special situations (IHTM04117 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04117)) that apply and the events are subject to a number of exceptions (IHTM04116 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04116)).

It is also possible for the same event to give rise to a double charge (IHTM04118 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04118)) under IHTA84/S32A, or to give rise to a charge under IHTA84/S32A and in connection with a pre-1976 death.

Where this applies, the Board of HMRC may choose which chargeable event to pursue, IHTA84/S33 (5)(c). You should refer to Heritage for advice. You should also ask them for advice where a pre-1976 death is involved.
