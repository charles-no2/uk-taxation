Compensation payments: compensation for personal injuries on death
Identifier: IHTM10263
Source: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10263
Published: 2016-03-20T09:28:26+00:00
Updated: 2023-06-29T13:45:30+01:00

Whether or not sums received by the estate for personal injuries should be brought into account depends on the nature of the payment received.

The following are taxable

- Payments under the Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1934 (IHTM10264 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10264))

The following are not taxable

- Compensation under the Fatal Accidents and National Insurance (Industrial Injuries) Acts (IHTM10265 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10265))

- Ex gratia payments for Tort committed by a member of the Armed Forces of the Crown or the United States’ Forces (IHTM10266 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10266))

- Ex gratia compensation payments made by the Criminal Injuries Compensation Authority (IHTM10267 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10267))

Special rules apply to sums received under the Damages (Scotland) Act 1976 (IHTM10268 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10268))
