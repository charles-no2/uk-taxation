Valuing property in an estate: general principles

The ‘estate’ (IHTM04029 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04029)) is defined in IHTA84/S5(1). In addition to the free estate it includes joint property (IHTM15011 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm15011)), settled property (IHTM16000 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm16000)) and gifts with reservation (GWR)(IHTM04071 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04071)) in which the deceased had a beneficial interest in possession.

In general you should look to value the different items of property (IHTM04030 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm04030)) in an estate separately (IHTM09715 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm09715)). For example, in a straightforward case you would look to value shares, the deceased’s house and car separately. But this does not stop you from valuing items of property together (IHTM09712 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm09712)) if the items are worth more together than if sold separately. This applies even if the property is held at separate titles or is left out of account (IHTM09713 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm09713)) in determining the chargeable estate for tax purposes.

You may also have to take account of the related property (IHTM09731 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm09731)) provisions under which property owned by a spouse/civil partner (IHTM11032 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm11032))or a charity may affect the valuation of property held in your deceased’s estate.
